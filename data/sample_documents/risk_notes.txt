These risk notes describe a fictional company, Northbridge Analytics, and a
fictional regulatory regime. All figures and named regulations are invented
for demonstration purposes only.

Customer concentration is flagged as a notable risk: the top 10 customers
are estimated to represent 34 percent of Northbridge Analytics' annual
recurring revenue, meaning the loss of even two or three large accounts
could materially affect reported growth rates.

Key-person dependency is also flagged. Several enterprise-sales
relationships are described internally as owned personally by the founding
CEO, Dana Whitfield, rather than by the broader sales organization, which
could pose continuity risk if that individual were to depart.

A fictional piece of legislation referred to internally as the Data
Handling Act of 2025 is noted as a compliance consideration for companies
in this space. Internal estimates suggest compliance work could increase
annual compliance-related costs by approximately 1.5 million dollars if the
legislation is enacted as currently drafted, though the notes stress this
figure is a preliminary internal estimate rather than a finalized budget.

Competitive risk is described as rising, largely from open-source
embedded-analytics toolkits that compete aggressively on price. Combined
with the 9 percent gross logo churn rate reported in company notes, the
risk notes recommend monitoring renewal pricing discipline closely over
the next two fiscal years.
